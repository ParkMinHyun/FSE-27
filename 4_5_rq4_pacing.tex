\subsection{RQ4: Pacing under Queue Pressure}
\label{sec:rq4}

\input{tables/tab_rq4_pacing_sizing.tex}

To examine how queue pressure and deadline margins affect pacing, we analyzed 3{,}050 pacing decisions recorded under \emph{Full} in the production-default 12MP normal condition on both devices.
Table~\ref{tab:rq4_pacing_sizing} groups these decisions by percentages of the Capture Timeout budget: Panel~(a) by the backlog \(B_i\) measured from the completed trace, and Panel~(b) by slack, the deadline margin at draft sequence completion.
The ratio \(\mathit{delay}_i/B_i\) includes only paced decisions (\(\mathit{delay}_i>0\)).

\myparagraph{Backlog Response}
Table~\ref{tab:rq4_pacing_sizing} shows that pacing activated more often as measured backlog increased, while its median delay relative to backlog decreased.
Activation increased from 0\% in the \(<10\%\) backlog group to 83.4\% in the \(\geq 50\%\) group.
Among paced decisions, the median \(\mathit{delay}_i/B_i\) fell from 29.1\% in the 10--30\% group to 8.3\% in the \(\geq 50\%\) group.
Note that this falling ratio describes relative delay and does not imply a decrease in absolute delay.

\myparagraph{Selective Pacing}
High measured backlog alone was not sufficient to trigger pacing.
In the \(\geq 50\%\) backlog group, 574 of 688 decisions were paced, leaving 114 unpaced.
Of these 114, 58 had no projected excess over the queue-level budget used by pacing and occurred predominantly at lower overheat levels.
For the other 56, an earlier admission skip had reduced the projected draft work to within this budget.

Pacing was more frequent among captures with less slack at draft completion, reaching 95.7\% in the \(<5\%\) slack group.
For the six unpaced captures in this group, predicted backlog underestimated measured backlog at the pacing decision.
However, later admission skips reduced draft work, and all six captures completed within budget.
These results indicate that pacing activates selectively, concentrating its delays on captures with high queue pressure and small deadline margins.
